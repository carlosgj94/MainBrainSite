AI Is Eating Software, but Embodiment May Eat the World

Published 2026-04-06 · Updated 2026-04-06 00:00:00 +0000 UTC

As software generation gets cheaper, the next scarce frontier may be the interface between intelligence and reality.

If AI is reducing the scarcity of software production, the next scarce frontier is likely to be the interface between intelligence and reality: robots, factories, labs, logistics, energy systems, and other embodied environments.

Software is becoming more generative, accessible, and compressible. Real-world systems remain stubborn because they involve safety, latency, hardware, regulation, and environmental messiness. That makes embodied and industrial engineering more attractive, not less, in an AI-native economy.

The more software becomes abundant, the more attractive physically anchored engineering looks.
