My current view is that software is not solved, but it is becoming less scarce at the level of implementation. AI is changing the economics of building digital products quickly enough that the strongest career leverage may move toward domains where intelligence still has to collide with reality.

That includes robotics, industrial systems, energy, hardware-software environments, infrastructure, and other high-accountability workflows. In these domains, coding still matters, but it is not the whole game. Safety, deployment, trust, integration, and physical constraints matter too.

This also explains why so many builders feel unsettled right now. The fear is not only job loss. It is the sense that the old ladder for becoming valuable may be changing. If AI compresses more junior and routine engineering work, then many people have to rethink how they compound.

The best answer may be to move toward scarce complements to intelligence: real systems, messy environments, domain knowledge, interdisciplinary judgment, and physical-world bottlenecks. That is one reason EE and robotics feel directionally right to me. They sit closer to the parts of the economy that still resist abstraction.

I do not think this means everyone should abandon software. It means software alone may be a weaker moat than software fused with reality.
